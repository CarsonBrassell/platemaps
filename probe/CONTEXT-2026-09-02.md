## Why the corpus is only half the city

5,019 records came from one OpenStreetMap snapshot (2026-08-18) and 675 from a
Yelp ranked search. OSM holds a business only if a volunteer entered it; Yelp
search returns a popularity-ranked sample, not an enumeration. **Both
under-represent the same thing** — neighbourhood bars, strip-mall taco shops,
anything recently opened.

This surfaced when Calvin named six restaurants he and Carson intended to demo
to investors and five were absent: Del Cerro Pizza and Beer, Clems Station, The
Duke Cocktails and Grub, Chamorro Grill, KNB Bistro (the sixth, The Other Side
Bar and Grill, is not in the county list either). **All five are in the county
permit list.** That is the failure mode in one line.

### The county permit list is the fix

Every legal food business holds a Department of Environmental Health permit, so
it is the only complete enumeration available. Free and public, via CivicData's
CKAN datastore. Already downloaded:

- `data/deh-facilities.json` — all 17,503 permit records (gitignored; `--refetch` re-pulls)
- `data/coverage-missing.json` — the 7,166 we lack, with name, address, city, ZIP
- `data/coverage-unverified.json` — the 516 to repair

The missing 7,166 by permit type: 4,925 Restaurant Food Facility · 1,146 Retail
Market with Deli · 582 Low Risk · 215 Single Operating Site · 212 Microenterprise
Home Kitchen · 86 satellite/restricted.

**Open product question:** which types to import. Restaurants are not in doubt.
A "Retail Market with Deli" is somewhere people eat lunch and there are 1,146 of
them. Microenterprise Home Kitchens are residential addresses and should stay out.

## What the plan has to solve

1. **Geocoding.** The county publishes address/city/ZIP but **no coordinates**,
   and the map needs them. Every existing record has lat/lng, so this is the one
   genuinely new piece of work. Options: US Census Bureau batch geocoder (free,
   10,000 per batch, untested here), Nominatim (free, ~1 req/sec, ~90 min for
   4,925), Google (~$5/1,000, fastest on suite addresses). Expect 5–10% to fail
   on plaza addresses — hold those, never guess.
2. **Import without duplicates.** Identity is `sourceKey` (`<source>:<id>`, see
   `src/lib/sourceKey.ts`); these would be `deh:<Record ID>`. Match each permit
   against the existing 5,695 on name tokens + parsed address before inserting —
   `scripts/verify-coverage.mjs` already contains that matcher.
3. **Repair what we hold.** 1,142 probable (adopt the county's address), 522 with
   no address at all (they *do* have coordinates — reverse geocode), 120
   unverified (hand-check; closures get `hold_reason`, never deletion).
4. **Menus for the new records.** Pipeline works, see `RUNBOOK.md`. A good night
   moves ~185 menus, so ~4,925 restaurants is roughly a month of nightly runs.
   **Constraint is the weekly usage limit, not money.**
5. **Production is stale.** The Vercel `DATABASE_URL` points at an old database,
   so **every dish is invisible in production.** Ten-minute fix, needs Calvin's
   Vercel login. Until then, demo from `localhost:3000`.
6. **Price hygiene.** 98.2% of 251,023 dishes are canonical `$12.00`. The rest:
   **1,913 priced `—`** (no price at all — not a menu by our own standard) and
   ~2,700 with a `+` suffix (real "from" prices that break sorting; needs a
   display decision).

### A consequence to plan for

Importing 4,925 restaurants drops menu coverage from **68.3% to about 34%**
overnight without losing a single menu. That is correct and expected. Decide
in advance which number is quoted: "68% of our restaurants have menus" is true
today but measured against a corpus missing half the city.

### Importer dedupe fallback — landed, and tightened

The fallback as first specified (same street number + street within 150 m, or
name score >= 0.5) was wrong 7 times in the first 10 pairs: `address()` in
verify-coverage strips suite numbers on purpose, so it matches any two
tenants of one plaza (Subway -> Kokoro, Firenze Trattoria -> Champagne Cafe),
and 0.5 is where "Julian Pie Company" meets "Julian Beer Company". **Do not
reuse `address()` to ask "is this the same business".** The importer now:
(1) treats a differing google_place_id on our row as a veto; (2) requires a
shared identifying word on the street-match branch; (3) needs name score
>= 0.8 on the name-only branch; (4) excludes held rows; (5) guards against two
permits landing on one row. On a 666-permit replay of the cache: 219 import,
91 duplicate by place id, 2 by fallback (both correct, both rows lacking a
place id), 14 closed, 199 not-food, 143 unmatched. The not-food (30%) and
unmatched (21%) shares are higher than planned; read the primaryType
breakdown and a sample of unmatched before trusting FOOD_TYPES or the match
rule. `--apply` still never run.
