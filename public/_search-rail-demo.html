Top row — switch, then a bare line

M's row without M's rail. The Discover/Friends switch keeps its position and its squared segmented design; the search loses its box entirely and becomes a rule with text sitting on it. Nothing between them — the row is held together by alignment, which is the same thing that holds the header nav together on the cream side.

Typing — ✕ appears

The one accessibility question. An unboxed field cannot take the standard orange outline-2 ring without drawing the box back around it. The focus indicator here is the rule itself — 1px muted to 2px ember-bright is a change of both thickness and color, and #ffb07a against the tiles clears 3:1 comfortably. That is a defensible reading of the floor, but it is a deliberate departure from "orange ring everywhere" and worth saying out loud before it ships.

03

Small screen — the crowding is gone

This is the case that forced today's awkward fallback, where the field drops to a second row and takes a left inset to dodge the zoom stack. With no box, the field simply wraps under the switch and spans the width. Nothing overlaps, and the row below the switch is no longer contested because the rule can sit above the zoom controls without colliding.

Why it fits now. The switch is ~190px of a ~340px map, so a second control on that line was never going to work. A boxed field wrapping underneath would have read as two stacked panels; a rule reads as one line of text, which is why the wrap is acceptable here and was not before.

The zoom stack drops to clear both rows. That is the same 40px push it already does, just measured from a taller header block.

Two things still open

How long is the line? Short keeps the map open but gives long restaurant names nowhere to go while typing.

DiscoverFriends Fly to a spot
long rule · 320px

Long reads as a header rule spanning toward the switch, and never truncates a name — but it is more ink across the top of the map.

The real risk, stated plainly. A rule at 32% white is legible over dark blocks and over water, but the map has bright arterials with orange glow pooled under them, and a hairline crossing one of those loses its edge. Mitigations, in order of preference: keep the field in the top-right where the tiles are typically darkest, lift the rest state to ~40% white, or give the row a very short vertical gradient scrim — which is the one thing that starts walking back toward a container, so it should be the last resort rather than the first.

05

If this is the one

Building it touches MapSearch.tsx (the whole chrome block, plus the responsive positioning comment that documents the old collision) and the map host where the switch is placed, since the two now share a row and must be laid out together rather than positioned independently. The dropdown is unchanged and out of scope — it keeps its dark panel, which is still correct: a results list needs a surface to sit on even when the field does not.
